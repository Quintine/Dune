# Richese Ambassador: paid acquisition and faction modifiers

Primary-source decision review, 7 September 2026. **Implement the paid draw as a purchase: Harkonnen receives its ordinary extra card when there is room, and another purchasing faction's payment follows Emperor income substitution. Ixian allied replacement additionally requires Bidding timing.** These are applications of general triggers, not claims that GF9 published an Ambassador-specific worked example. The earlier Ambassador audits' unresolved acquisition classification and categorical bank-only recommendation should not remain implementation blockers.

## Classification and beneficiary coverage

Exchanging a fixed spice payment for a new deck card is a purchase in the ordinary meaning of the general acquisition rule. The verb _draw_ specifies where the card comes from; it does not say the acquisition is free, exclude a purchase, or suppress other factions' advantages. Consequently, apply the existing Harkonnen purchase benefit. Do not reinterpret the separate, unpaid Ixian Ambassador discard-and-replace effect as another purchase.

Emperor's exact operative sentence on printed base p.19, under **BIDDING**, is: “Whenever any other faction pays spice for a Treachery Card, they pay it to you instead of the Spice Bank.” The heading names the advantage; the sentence contains no express phase condition. This differs from the Ixian sentence's express timing clause. Do not silently insert a Bidding-only condition into Emperor's operative trigger.

Likewise, the bank instruction is not an explicit exception to Emperor's replacement of the bank as recipient. Ordinary shipping and revival instructions also name the bank before faction income substitutions apply. The FAQ's extra-revival example is particularly useful: even direct bank wording can coexist with such a substitution. Treating this paid draw as a purchase for Harkonnen but categorically not a card payment for Emperor would require an exception absent from the retrieved sources. This recommendation is also consistent with the project's existing [Richese special-purchase composition](RICHESE_ACQUISITION_RULES.md).

For the default two-faction alliance rules, the complete beneficiary matrix is:

The buyer is the chosen beneficiary: it pays and receives the card. The owner does not buy a card and then gift it to the beneficiary. Retain each player's actual hand limit and currently available resources; do not hardcode a universal four-card cap in an effect helper.

## Trigger authority and an unavailable private beneficiary

E3 p.8 assigns Ecaz the choice to give its ally the benefit of a triggered effect. Base p.12 leaves allied assistance to the particular player-sheet abilities. Neither adds an ally acceptance right. The Richese instruction contains no internal optional purchase or refusal branch. Therefore, do not offer an affordable beneficiary a new **Buy / Decline** choice after consuming the token, and do not invent a mandatory pre-trigger permission dialog. Ecaz can leave the physical Ambassador in place at its original offer.

For an ally's private inability, the recommended resolution is: Ecaz commits the otherwise legal trigger and beneficiary; the server attempts the conditional acquisition; if there is insufficient available spice, no hand space, or no drawable card after normal recycling, no payment or card occurs and the triggered token remains used. Do not partially debit the price. No Emperor income or Harkonnen bonus arises without the primary purchase. The printed trigger conditions concern the entry and token, whereas the Richese effect itself conditions acquisition on room; those are separate checks.

This is an explicit implementation interpretation of an unavailable effect, not a quoted publisher failed-cost rule. It preserves the printed optional trigger, required price and conditional acquisition without inventing either a beneficiary veto or resources. CHOAM's separately printed allowance to discard any number of cards is not, by itself, proof of a universal zero-effect rule. A future contrary publisher clarification would warrant revisiting the unavailable-purchase branch, not the purchase-modifier classification.

The privacy consequence matters: do not expose an ally's affordability or fullness as a free projected eligible/blocked answer to Ecaz. Ecaz may receive its own private eligibility explanation. An actual committed effect may publicly succeed or fail; that unavoidable outcome is different from arbitrarily probing another player's secret holdings without consuming the opportunity. Public failure wording should not distinguish spice shortage, a full hand, or hidden card identities.

## Ixian timing is a positive requirement

The real ordinary movement, shipment and worm-entry callers resolve outside Bidding. They therefore do not offer Ixian allied replacement, even if Ecaz is allied with Ixians. Do not delay the draw until Bidding to create eligibility. Do not create a fictitious auction receipt merely to reach the current replacement implementation.

If a separately implemented, legal entry effect eventually triggers this Ambassador during Bidding, evaluate the literal timing requirement at that actual event. For Ecaz with an Ixian ally, such a paid deck acquisition meets the general purchase-and-timing conditions; a new direct Ambassador FAQ is not needed. This conditional statement does not assert that the current engine has such an entry producer. A Richese-family card recycled into the ordinary deck is still being acquired from that deck; the Ambassador does not become a sale from Richese's separate cache.

The same timing distinction matters for funds. Existing allied shipment aid is not general-purpose available spice. The FAQ's Bidding purchase-funding permission does not authorize spending movement-phase shipment escrow on this purchase. Use the beneficiary's available spice in current non-Bidding entry paths; compose any separate funding permission explicitly.

## Runtime integration implications

Current line anchors are navigation aids, not rules evidence. `ambassadorEffectBlock` near `game/engine.ts:6261` still blocks Richese through the implemented-effect list. `resolveAmbassadorEffect` near6396 and `finishAmbassador` near6372 are the real acquisition/resume boundary. `draw` near2067 already owns deck replenishment. It must execute once after commitment, never while constructing public eligibility or probing a cancellation.

The shared auction machinery cannot be called unchanged. `settleAuction` near4690 and `settleRicheseLot` near4141 create actual sale receipts. `continueAuctionSale`/`auctionBonus` near4804 subsequently reach `nextAuction`, while the Ixian response near10627 requires `currentAuctionSale` and stages a replacement discard with auction provenance. The new effect must keep its Ambassador event and resume destination rather than advance any auction or phase. Reuse narrowly shared benefit calculations, with an explicitly different acquisition continuation.

Suggested deterministic sequence: validate the public entry/token/beneficiary authority; commit the trigger; evaluate the acquisition using the beneficiary's private resources; if executable, commit payment and the first physical draw, then settle applicable income and bonus responses; otherwise record the unavailable effect without payment. Finish the Ambassador and its original entry once in either case. The first card's identity remains private. Do not make public availability enumerate a hidden top card. Harkonnen cancellation affects the extra acquisition, not the already purchased card; Emperor cancellation affects income, not the completed purchase. Existing choice-free automatic continuation should apply when its prerequisites are satisfied.

Meaningful evidence should include all beneficiary rows, Harkonnen near its cap, Emperor present/absent/self/canceled, Ecaz–Ixians outside Bidding, recycled deck cards, no-card supply, exact-once JSON/SQLite recovery, and the original worm/movement suffix. Test an actual Bidding entry only when a real producer supports it. Do not fabricate one merely to obtain coverage.

## Remaining scope

No material source conflict was found that requires withholding the acquisition modifiers for these beneficiaries. The unavailable-effect behavior above is identified separately as an interpretation, rather than silently presented as explicit text. The lack of a named worked example is not such a conflict. Optional Homeworld, Nexus, skill and nonstandard-alliance modifiers need their own existing module contracts; this audit does not silently certify them. Competing arrival reactions, future Bidding entry producers and safe nested response/discard persistence are implementation work, not reasons to redefine the purchase trigger.

This review changes no runtime or tests and does not establish complete Ecaz or expansion-game compliance.
